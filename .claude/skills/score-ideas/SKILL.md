Apply master protocol §9 to every candidate currently in `company/research/opportunity_map.md`,
extended per `CLAUDE.md`'s mission focus on viral / product-led growth.

1. For each candidate, score 0–10 on: Pain Severity, Frequency, Market Size, Willingness to Pay, Competition Gap, Technical Feasibility, Differentiation, Defensibility, Growth Potential, AI Leverage, Distribution Potential, **and Viral Mechanism Strength**.
2. Viral Mechanism Strength is scored against the specific mechanism recorded in the candidate's opportunity-map entry — not a general vibe of "this feels shareable." Score low (0–3) if the entry has no falsifiable mechanism, or only a generic one ("people will tell their friends"); mid (4–6) for a real but weak or easily-copied mechanism; high (7–10) only for a mechanism with some actual evidence behind it — a comparable product that demonstrably spread this way, or a structural reason the product doesn't fully work without a second user. This is a semi-gating dimension for this company: a candidate scoring 3 or below here should not advance to `contrarian-attack` no matter how the other ten dimensions look, since the mission focus makes it disqualifying on its own — record why in `company/research/rejected_ideas.md` rather than silently dropping it.
3. Calculate an overall opportunity score (state the method you used — e.g. simple average, or a weighted one — so it's reproducible, and keep it consistent across candidates). Don't let a weighting scheme quietly wash out a low Viral Mechanism Strength score with high scores elsewhere — surface it separately in the ranked list, not just folded into one number.
4. For every candidate, write a genuine "WHY THIS SCORE MAY BE WRONG" note — the specific way this score could be misleading, not a generic disclaimer. For the viral dimension specifically, this is often "the comparable case might not generalize" or "this could be survivorship bias in the source." If you can't think of one, that itself is worth noting (it may mean you need the contrarian agent's help, not that the score is unusually solid).
5. Write the ranked list back to `company/research/opportunity_map.md`, highest score first, each with its full score breakdown (Viral Mechanism Strength shown on its own, not buried in the average) and the "why this may be wrong" note visible next to it.
6. Do not treat a high score as sufficient justification on its own. Before any candidate moves toward selection, run the `contrarian-attack` skill against it.
